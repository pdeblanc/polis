# Birds 2 Project Plan

## 1. Purpose

Birds 2 is the next iteration of the Polis moving-population model.

Birds 1 demonstrated that a population can be represented through deterministic random-access cell and flow queries while preserving exact integer conservation, canonical stochastic ownership, query-order independence, and finite recursive dependencies.

Birds 2 will retain those core ideas while changing several important parts of the model:

- replace Birds 1's fixed deterministic coarse initialization with an outward expansion hierarchy;
- integrate outward expansion with inward refinement;
- compose the global population from independently generated time-zero origin channels;
- reorganize where channel contributions are summed so that the hierarchy can evaluate them efficiently;
- calibrate the resulting expansion/refinement process against the Poisson–Brownian reference;
- terminate aggregate population refinement at a materialization frontier and continue below it with explicit individual paths;
- reconsider the production multinomial sampler, including the Poisson-race construction developed during the Birds 1 investigation; and
- perform another round of end-to-end profiling and optimization after the new architecture is substantially complete.

Birds 2 will be added **alongside** the existing Birds model. Birds 1 remains available as a working reference implementation and comparison target. A later project may decide to remove Birds 1, but Birds 2 does not require or imply that removal.

---

## 2. General development principles

Birds 2 should preserve the project-wide Polis invariants:

- deterministic addressed randomness;
- query-order independence;
- cache independence;
- exact integer conservation within the supported numeric domain;
- one canonical owner for each stochastic fact;
- finite dependency enumeration for supported queries;
- model-specific recursive structures rather than a premature generic hierarchy framework.

The existing Birds model should remain usable throughout Birds 2 development.

Birds 2 may reuse shared utilities from Birds 1 where their semantics remain appropriate, but it should not contort the new design merely to maximize code sharing.

Where Birds 2 introduces materially different stochastic semantics, it should use its own model implementation and tests.

### Single-origin model, summed model, and materialization wrapper

Treat the construction as three models:

- **Birds 2a** is the single-origin expansion/refinement model. Its population and flow queries describe contributions from one time-zero origin channel.
- **Birds 2b** is the summed model, composing origin channels into the global population, including optimizations to the hierarchy placement of summation. Its population and flow query interfaces may follow Birds 1, and it supports fine-resolution queries without knowing whether it is being wrapped.
- **Birds 2c** wraps 2b with a materialization frontier and an explicit path model. It queries 2b at the frontier or coarser scales and defines its own finer-scale answers from materialized birds.

Birds 2c is not required to reproduce 2b's unused fine-scale answers, including finer facts retained by expansion within its channels. It must respect the populations and flows it takes from 2b at the frontier and remain internally consistent under its own semantics.

The wrapper's query cutoff is not necessarily a cutoff on 2b's internal dependencies: answering a frontier query may require finer retained expansion facts inside its channels. Materialization does not require changing the underlying 2a or 2b models or making them aware of the wrapper.

### Cost-benefit decisions

Optimization and calibration work should include explicit cost-benefit analysis. A proposed change may be implemented, deferred, or declined with a documented rationale and supporting evidence. Completion does not require every investigated optimization to succeed or every observed discrepancy to be eliminated; structural correctness remains mandatory.

## 3. Phase 1 — Expansion and contraction integration

Begin with the current single-origin expansion construction.

Use **length 1 and duration 1** as the Birds 2 base initialization, rather than inheriting Birds 1's length 16 and duration 256. Expansion supplies coarser scales as needed. The scale relation remains `T=L²`, so a channel introduced at a larger origin width uses the corresponding squared initial duration. Keep these conventions in named constants. This choice does not by itself select the complete time-zero origin partition.

Complete the single-channel Birds 2a model by integrating the retained expansion facts with the inward refinement machinery.

The combined hierarchy must define, for every supported query, whether the requested fact is:

- directly generated by expansion;
- a projection or aggregation of retained expansion records; or
- generated by ordinary inward refinement below the available expansion detail.

Expansion-generated facts are authoritative. Inward refinement must not independently resample a stochastic decision already determined by expansion.

Single-origin expansion retains no additional provenance beyond Birds 1's temporal construction: sum legs with matching endpoints before subdividing again. In particular, generate in order `0 → T → 4T → 2T → 3T`, aggregating `2T → 4T` flows over their `T` sources before splitting at `3T`. Any contraction dependency requiring an association not determined by the retained three-time batches must receive an explicit consistent construction, not an invented projection.

The single-channel model should support random-access queries at finer spatial and temporal resolutions than those explicitly generated during outward expansion.

This phase should also complete the single-channel dependency/support analysis needed to prove that such queries have finite ancestry.

Implementation status: `src/models/birds2a` contains the unit-scale expansion, cell/flow entry classifiers, expansion-fact readers, and cell-subdivision batch classification. The remaining owner classifiers, support enumeration, and contraction query engine are not yet implemented. See the [expansion implementation notes](./BIRDS_EXPANSION_PROTOTYPE.md) for details; Phase 1 is not complete.

### Phase 1 completion criteria

- One origin channel supports both outward expansion and inward refinement.
- Expansion and refinement agree exactly wherever their constructions overlap.
- Queries terminate at retained expansion facts without relying on evaluation history.
- Conservation and canonical stochastic ownership remain intact.
- Further outward expansion does not change already-defined answers.

## 4. Phase 2 — Global channel composition

Extend the single-channel construction to the full spatial population in Birds 2b.

Choose a canonical disjoint partition of the time-zero spatial line into origin channels. Each channel receives an independent random namespace and its own time-zero population.

A global population or flow query is initially defined as the sum of every origin-channel contribution that can affect the requested fact.

The completed single-channel support analysis must provide a finite way to enumerate all possible contributing channels for a supported global query.

This explicit sum is the reference global construction.

### Hierarchy placement of summation

Once the explicit global sum works, optimize the hierarchy by moving summations earlier where appropriate.

Instead of independently refining every contributing channel all the way to the requested output and adding only at the end, compatible contributions may be combined at higher hierarchy levels so that later refinement work operates on aggregated populations and flows.

These transformations must continue to approximate the same Poisson–Brownian target, but need not preserve the explicit channel sum's exact probability law. Small differences are acceptable: for example, applying a bounded-distribution cutoff after summation rather than before it may produce narrower support. Document and evaluate changes to support, joint distributions, and conditional behavior, while retaining conditioning information needed for consistency and conservation.

The optimized construction may produce different outcomes for a given seed than the explicit channel-sum reference construction. Birds 2 does not require structural changes to preserve the previous construction's seeded outcomes.

The explicit channel-sum implementation remains useful as a conceptual and statistical reference when evaluating alternative hierarchy placements.

### Phase 2 completion criteria

- Global queries compose a finite set of disjoint origin channels.
- The explicit channel-sum implementation is correct and reproducible.
- Useful summation placements have been evaluated and adopted where justified, with deferrals or declined changes documented through cost-benefit analysis.
- The optimized construction has explicitly specified stochastic semantics and preserves the required conservation, ownership, consistency, and finite-dependency invariants.

---

## 5. Phase 3 — Statistical calibration

After the expansion/refinement hierarchy and summation placement are stable, calibrate Birds 2 against the intended Poisson–Brownian reference process.

Calibration should proceed through the model's dependency construction from earlier facts toward later derived facts.

For representative canonical cells and flows, compare sampled count distributions across many seeds with their corresponding Poisson target distributions.

Poisson marginals organize this dependency-ordered calibration work; they are not the only metrics for final model quality. Broader evaluation includes joint population behavior, displacement moments, temporal dependence, and trajectories, using the model-independent [evaluation scorecard](./BIRDS_EVALUATION.md). Define the measurement scope and practical acceptance criteria before the calibration campaign, and reassess relevant metrics after materialization or sampler changes.

Where a stage introduces systematic discrepancy, investigate changes to its stochastic kernel or coupling before attributing downstream errors to later stages.

Calibration may adjust:

- expansion kernels;
- bridge kernels;
- variance parameters;
- within-cell approximations;
- spatial subdivision or reconciliation couplings;
- other stochastic details of the hierarchy.

The purpose is not to make every finite-resolution observable exactly Brownian. Birds 2 remains a hierarchical approximation. The goal is to understand and substantially reduce avoidable bias before the model transitions to explicit individuals.

Calibration changes must preserve the structural invariants of the model, especially conservation and canonical ownership.

### Phase 3 completion criteria

- Important count-distribution biases within the agreed measurement scope have been investigated, with findings and unresolved causes documented.
- Corrections have been implemented and characterized where worthwhile; deferred or declined corrections have an explicit cost-benefit rationale.
- The resulting aggregate model has a documented relationship to its Poisson–Brownian reference.
- Remaining discrepancies are explicit rather than accidental.

## 6. Phase 4 — Materialization frontier

Introduce a finite refinement frontier in the Birds 2c wrapper at which anonymous aggregate populations become explicit individual birds. Birds 2a and 2b remain independently queryable and do not implement this frontier.

Choose the frontier from the model parameters and a target expected population per materialization cell, rather than from the realized population of each individual cell.

An expected-population target is not a hard bound on realized population or materialization cost.

Materialization begins at the start of a normal refinement epoch, where cell populations and flow counts already exist at some scale `(L, T)`.

The initial materialization construction has four stages:

### A. Materialize positions

Each cell samples one independent uniform spatial position within its interval for every bird in its population.

### B. Allocate positions to inflows

Each cell uniformly samples an assignment of its materialized birds to its known incoming flows, respecting all inflow counts exactly.

### C. Allocate positions to outflows

Independently of the inflow assignment, each cell uniformly samples an assignment of the same materialized birds to its outgoing flows, respecting all outflow counts exactly.

### D. Match flow endpoints

Each flow samples a uniformly random bijection between the materialized source birds assigned to that flow and its materialized destination birds.

After Stage D, each resulting endpoint pair defines an explicit path segment.

These uniform choices define the initial implementation. After correctness is established, non-uniform position, allocation, or matching distributions may be investigated as model improvements.

Birds 2c performs no further anonymous population subdivision below the materialization frontier and does not request 2b's fine-scale answers as constraints on its paths.

---

## 7. Individual path refinement

Below the materialization frontier, hand each explicit path segment to a separate path-refinement model.

Given a segment with known start and end positions, one refinement operation:

1. selects the halftime;
2. samples a bounded Brownian-bridge midpoint displacement using the truncated-normal convention;
3. creates the two child segments.

This operation can repeat recursively to arbitrary supported precision.

The path model should have canonical addressed midpoint randomness and should not depend on the order in which path segments are refined.

Materialized birds therefore have persistent explicit trajectories below the frontier, while birds above it remain anonymous aggregate population units.

All finer-scale population and flow answers exposed by 2c must be derived from these paths, not independently sampled from 2b. Derive path-support bounds that permit finite enumeration of candidate segments for such queries: a bridge can enter a queried region even when both endpoints lie outside it. Verify frontier agreement, fine-query/path consistency, finite candidate enumeration, and query-order/cache independence.

This should substantially simplify identity tracking relative to Birds 1.

## 8. Phase 5 — End-to-end profiling and performance work

Once expansion, optimized channel composition, calibration, materialization, and path refinement are integrated, perform a new end-to-end simulation profiling pass.

Birds 2 will have substantially different workloads from Birds 1, so performance conclusions from the Birds 1 implementation should not be assumed to transfer.

Use profiling to identify the dominant costs in actual model and visualization workloads, then optimize the expensive paths.

Performance work may include changes to:

- hierarchy evaluation;
- caching;
- classifier and geometry operations;
- channel summation;
- distribution sampling;
- materialization;
- path refinement;
- query batching;
- rendering or viewport coordination.

Optimization should follow measured bottlenecks rather than a predetermined list.

---

## 9. Phase 6 — Multinomial sampler decision

Reevaluate the Poisson-race multinomial sampler after the Birds 2 hierarchy and materialization frontier have changed the workload.

Birds 1 retained the sequential-binomial production sampler because the Poisson-race implementation was slower on the workloads measured at the time, despite having a stronger desirable coupling property.

Birds 2 should benchmark the alternatives again using representative end-to-end workloads from the new model.

If the performance cost is acceptable, prefer the sampler with the stronger stochastic coupling semantics.

If the Poisson-race construction remains prohibitively expensive, investigate whether it can be optimized or whether the production sampler should remain unchanged.

The final choice should be an explicit Birds 2 model decision rather than an accidental inheritance from Birds 1.

## 10. User interface

Birds 2 should receive its own demo page and should coexist with the Birds 1 demo.

UI development is intentionally opportunistic.

The project should expect to make interface changes as the model becomes usable and its inspection needs become clearer, but there is no fixed list or schedule of required UI changes beyond providing enough interface to explore and evaluate Birds 2.

Possible changes may arise from:

- new expansion-scale navigation;
- comparisons between Birds 1 and Birds 2;
- materialized trajectory inspection;
- calibration experiments;
- performance diagnostics;
- newly useful model parameters or debug views.

Such changes should be made when interaction with the model reveals a concrete need.

---

## 11. Evaluation and regression strategy

Birds 1 remains an important reference during Birds 2 development.

Birds 2 tests should separately cover:

- structural correctness and conservation;
- expansion/refinement overlap;
- channel composition;
- stochastic calibration;
- materialization consistency;
- explicit path refinement;
- query-order and cache independence;
- finite dependency ancestry;
- performance regressions where stable measurements are useful.

Birds 1 and Birds 2 need not produce identical stochastic worlds for the same seed. Comparisons between them should focus on model behavior, statistical quality, query complexity, and user-visible capabilities rather than result equality.

Likewise, structural revisions within Birds 2 need not preserve the exact seeded worlds produced by earlier Birds 2 prototypes. Each accepted model structure must instead be deterministic and internally consistent under its own semantics.

---

## 12. Project sequence

The intended Birds 2 sequence is:

1. **Expansion**
   - complete the single-channel Birds 2a expansion model;
   - integrate inward refinement.

2. **Global composition**
   - build Birds 2b by summing a finite set of origin channels;
   - optimize where summation occurs in the hierarchy.

3. **Calibration**
   - measure aggregate cell and flow distributions against Poisson targets;
   - correct important expansion/refinement biases.

4. **Materialization**
   - wrap the summed Birds 2b model with Birds 2c's fixed materialization frontier;
   - construct explicit endpoint identities and flow mappings;
   - hand off to individual path refinement.

5. **End-to-end profiling**
   - profile the completed Birds 2 architecture;
   - optimize the measured bottlenecks.

6. **Multinomial sampler**
   - reevaluate the Poisson-race sampler under Birds 2 workloads;
   - adopt, optimize, or explicitly decline it.

UI improvements may occur throughout these phases whenever practical experience with the model reveals a useful change.

---

## 13. Birds 2 completion

Birds 2 is complete when:

- outward expansion replaces Birds 1's fixed deterministic coarse initialization;
- global queries compose the spatial population through a finite origin-channel hierarchy;
- expansion and inward refinement form one consistent random-access model;
- major aggregate statistical biases have been measured and calibrated where practical;
- Birds 2c hands off from aggregate queries to explicit paths at a deterministic materialization frontier, without requiring Birds 2a or 2b to stop supporting finer queries;
- explicit birds below that frontier have recursively refinable paths;
- the multinomial-sampler choice has been revisited using Birds 2 workloads;
- end-to-end profiling has been performed and major practical bottlenecks addressed where worthwhile, with remaining limitations and cost-benefit decisions documented;
- the model has an explorable browser demo; and
- Birds 1 remains available alongside Birds 2 unless a separate later decision removes it.

Birds 2 should leave behind not only a better moving-population demo, but a more general Polis pattern:

```text
coarse stochastic expansion
        ↓
aggregate random-access refinement
        ↓
materialization frontier
        ↓
explicit individual histories
```

That pattern is intended to inform later Polis models containing animals, people, settlements, and other populations that exist as aggregates at large scales and as individuals only when sufficiently refined.
